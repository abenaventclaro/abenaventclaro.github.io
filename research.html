Current research

How does rain remove dirt?

Right now, I am studying the physics of particle detachment from surfaces under continuous water flow, with solar-panel cleaning as the motivating problem.

The question

Dust and other particles accumulate on solar panels and reduce their performance. Rain offers a natural cleaning mechanism, but the physical conditions under which attached particles detach are still not fully understood.

What I am studying right now

Right now, I am investigating this problem by combining controlled microfluidic experiments with analytical modelling. The key idea is that rain cleaning is not only a droplet-impact problem: on a tilted surface, an advancing air-water interface first reaches a particle and a flowing water film then acts on it.

Why it matters

My goal is to identify the mechanisms and critical conditions for particle removal, from ideal model particles to more realistic contaminants. Beyond solar panels, this work addresses a broader question in interfacial fluid dynamics: how moving interfaces and fluid stresses interact with particles attached to surfaces.

Research themes

The current project sits at the intersection of several areas that have shaped my work.

Fluid dynamics & microfluidics

Confined flows, liquid-gas fronts, pressure-driven motion and physically grounded analytical models.

Interfaces, wetting & capillarity

Moving contact lines, imbibition, coated surfaces and capillary forces at small scales.

Multiphase & oscillatory flows

Coupled liquid-gas dynamics, bubble-driven oscillations and damping in confined geometries.

Rheology & particle-flow interactions

Flow-based measurement, transport through complex media and the mechanics of particle detachment.
